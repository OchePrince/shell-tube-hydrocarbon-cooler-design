# Hydrocarbon Product Cooler: Shell-and-Tube Design Report

Design of a shell-and-tube cooler for 12 kg/s of hydrocarbon (150 to 90 °C) using cooling water, with hand design, Aspen Plus V14 HeatX/EDR validation, a sensitivity study and a robustness test.

## Summary

EDR's own Design (Sizing) result is the selected exchanger: 116 tubes of 0.75 in OD, 4.88 m long, one pass in a 13.27 in AES shell, giving about 13% excess area with 0.12 bar shell-side and 0.11 bar tube-side pressure drop, at $24,346 in EDR.

- It cools 12 kg/s of hydrocarbon from 150 to 90 °C against cooling water entering at 30 °C (water flow 28.7 kg/s).
- The hand-built designs also pass every limit but cost more: Design B (317 tubes, 2.44 m, 14.8% excess) is $28,174 and Design A (168 tubes, 3.66 m, 12.6%) is $28,144. B has the lowest pressure drops (0.093 and 0.149 bar).
- Design B still passes with a five-alkane hydrocarbon mixture (12.85% excess). The selected EDR design has not yet been run with that mixture.

## Design basis and deviations from the brief

The brief asks for a hydrocarbon cooler: 12 kg/s from 150 to 90 °C at 5.0 bar, cooled by water entering at 30 °C and 3.0 bar, with allowable pressure drops of 0.40 bar (hydrocarbon) and 0.70 bar (water). The brief was generated by ChatGPT, so some inputs were changed to realistic values and Aspen's own properties were used.

| Item | Brief | Used here | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cooling water flow | max 20 kg/s | 28.7 kg/s | 20 kg/s would give a 51.5 °C outlet; 28.7 kg/s meets the 45 °C target |
| Hydrocarbon fouling | 0.00035 m²K/W | 0.0002 m²K/W | Clean light product; 0.00035 is heavy-oil fouling |
| Cooling water fouling | 0.00018 m²K/W | 0.00018 m²K/W | Unchanged |
| Shell-side viscosity correction | wall viscosity 0.55 mPa·s | 1.0 | Wall viscosity is below bulk, which is backwards for a cooled fluid |
| Heat duty | 1800 kW | 1570 kW (pseudo-component), 1863 kW (alkane mixture) | Aspen's hydrocarbon properties differ from the brief's |
| Hydrocarbon properties at 150 °C | ρ 780, Cp 2.50, μ 0.85 mPa·s, k 0.115 | ρ 753, Cp 2.18 (average), μ 0.59 mPa·s, k 0.099 | Aspen's single pseudo-component (NBP about 270 °C, SG about 0.85) |

The brief names no hydrocarbon, so Aspen models it as one pseudo-component. The robustness test uses a five-alkane mixture whose duty (1863 kW) is close to the brief's 1800 kW.

Water is on the tube side and the hydrocarbon on the shell side. The property method is IDEAL; PENG-ROB gave a wrong liquid-water heat capacity.

## Hand design and the three candidates

Three hand designs were sized with the Kern method on the shell side, Dittus-Boelter on the tube side and the Coulson bundle-diameter correlation. The log-mean temperature difference is 80.4 °C and the correction factor about 0.98.

The shell side is the bottleneck: the hydrocarbon film coefficient (about 1,380 W/m²K by Kern) is far below the water side.

| | Design A | Design B | Design C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tube OD | 0.75 in (14 BWG) | 0.625 in (16 BWG) | 0.75 in |
| Tube passes | 2 | 2 | 1 (water about 1.25 m/s) |
| Tubes in 18 in shell | 168 | 310 | not built in EDR |
| Length | 3.66 m | 2.44 m | not built in EDR |

C was carried only as a hand design. The EDR pass-count test showed one pass drops B to 4.8% excess, below the 10% target, so C was not taken into EDR.

## Aspen Plus and EDR validation setup

HeatX block, Rigorous Shell & Tube (EDR), Rating/Checking mode, geometry typed in by hand.

- Streams: hydrocarbon 12 kg/s, 150 °C, 5 bar, outlet 90 °C; cooling water 28.71 kg/s, 30 °C, 3 bar.
- Components: H2O plus one hydrocarbon pseudo-component; property method IDEAL.
- EDR process inputs: fouling 0.0002 (hot) and 0.00018 (cold) m²K/W; allowable pressure drop 0.40 bar (shell) and 0.70 bar (tubes).
- Final design B: AES, hot fluid shell side, 18 in shell, 317 tubes, 0.625 in OD, 0.065 in wall, 0.8125 in triangular pitch, 96 in long, 2 passes, single segmental baffles, 25% cut, 6.5 in spacing (11 baffles), end spaces chosen by EDR.

Setup lessons: leftover baffle inlet and end-space inputs inflated B's excess area; a leftover 0.083 in wall raised the tube pressure drop; the inlet end space needs about 9.7 in for the 6.065 in nozzle.

## EDR Design (Sizing) result

Letting EDR size the exchanger itself gave a smaller, cheaper design than the hand-built ones: $24,346 against $28,174 for B, with about 13% excess area.

The run used EDR's Design (Sizing) mode with AES, hot fluid on the shell side, 0.75 in tubes on 0.9375 in pitch, the same fouling and allowable pressure drops as before, a minimum-cost basis, and a minimum excess area of 10%. A first run with no margin set gave 4% excess ($21,704, using 91–92% of the allowed pressure drops), so it was rejected. A second run with tube length fixed at 192 in (4.88 m, one of the brief's standard lengths) is the result below.

| | EDR Design (4.88 m) | Design B | Design A |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shell ID | 13.27 in | 18 in | 18 in |
| Tubes × length | 116 × 4.88 m | 317 × 2.44 m | 168 × 3.66 m |
| Tube passes | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Baffle cut | 37.7% | 25% | 25% |
| Excess area | about 13% | 14.8% | 12.6% |
| Shell ΔP (limit 0.40 bar) | 0.12 bar | 0.093 bar | 0.146 bar |
| Tube ΔP (limit 0.70 bar) | 0.11 bar | 0.149 bar | 0.285 bar |
| Total cost | $24,346 | $28,174 | $28,144 |

EDR's design has a smaller shell and one pass, so it uses 32.4 m² of surface against B's 35.1 m², and the water velocity is 1.47 m/s with no vibration or RhoV2 problem.

## Final selection, justification and risks

The EDR Design-mode exchanger is selected because it meets every limit at the lowest cost: $24,346 against $28,174 for Design B.

- Compliance: 116 tubes of 0.75 in, 4.88 m, one pass, 13.27 in shell; about 13% excess area, shell ΔP 0.12 bar and tube ΔP 0.11 bar (limits 0.40 and 0.70), tube velocity 1.47 m/s, no vibration problem.
- Cost: 14% below B and 13% below A.
- Size: 13.27 in shell against 18 in, and 32.4 m² of surface against B's 35.1 m².
- Runner-up: Design B (14.8% excess, 0.093 and 0.149 bar, 2.44 m long) if margin and a shorter exchanger matter more than cost.

Risks and trade-offs:

- The selected design has not been run with the five-alkane mixture; B lost about 2 points of margin in that test, so the roughly 13% margin should be rechecked.
- A 4.88 m tube needs about that much clear space at the exchanger end to pull the bundle, and with one pass any fouling inside the tubes cuts straight into the margin.
- The 37.7% baffle cut and 0.52 m/s shell velocity are EDR's cost-minimum choices; B's tighter 25% cut gives the lower pressure drop.
- EDR's pressure drop is much lower than the hand (Kern) value, so the hand number should not be quoted as the design value.
- Results rest on Aspen's pseudo-component (duty 1570 kW) rather than the brief's 1800 kW.
